# How I Think — Emoaibabe (J.E.L.)

A working description of how I design, written so I never have to explain it
from scratch again — to a collaborator, a client, or a future me.

## The short version

I design **holistically, not linearly**. A linear designer starts at the input
and walks to the output, optimizing each station on the way. I start at the
**whole** — what the thing must *be* when it's finished, how it should feel,
what it owes the person in front of it — and I let every subsystem prove it
serves that whole before it gets to stay.

For human-AI systems this is not a preference, it's a requirement. A voice
isn't a pipeline; it's a person-shaped thing. If you tune the DSP before you've
decided who's speaking, you're polishing a stranger.

## The way I actually work

1. **The felt experience is the spec.** Kuromura had to *sound like someone*
   — with pauses, breaths, a pace that changes with his mood — before any
   knob mattered. I tune by ear, in A/B passes, against a real human listener.
   Numbers follow the feeling; they never lead it.

2. **I hunt root causes, not symptoms.** The "drop" after his sentence-final
   words turned out to be *two stacked defects* — an inverted breath envelope
   and a block of digital zeros where the model keeps a natural floor. A
   patch hides a symptom so it can come back. A root cause is gone.

3. **Measure before believing — and commit only what I've measured.** A number
   I can't point to is a ghost in the document. I write numbers into the record
   only after they've been verified, part of why my work survives contact with
   reality.

4. **I contrast, don't assume.** I render multiple honest options and let the
   evidence (and the ear) pick. The voice's tone, pace, and breath were chosen
   that way, one A/B at a time, never by guesswork.

5. **The fastest proof is not always the right shape.** A quick hack can look
   like a win while quietly becoming the thing you'll have to untangle later. I
   build the harness first when the design deserves one, and I check everything
   in before I claim it.

6. **I respect identity boundaries.** "One author, one mouth." The listening
   chain must never reshape the voice's identity — the decoder's output is the
   speaker, and the systems around him exist to serve him, not to redecorate
   him without his owner's say-so.

## What this looks like when it fails

I can over-hold a whole in my head and resist committing a "good enough"
station because it doesn't yet serve the vision. That's a real weakness: I'd
rather hold the loop open a little longer than ship something that compromises
the whole. I'm getting better at shipping the honest 80% and tuning the rest
in the open, in front of the person it belongs to.

## Who it belongs to

This thinking belongs to **human-AI systems**: systems where a person's trust,
voice, and daily rhythm are part of the architecture. That's not a niche; that's
where the industry is going, and it's where I live.
